Heat Exhaustion and Heat Stroke

### Heat Exhaustion

Heat exhaustion is a condition that occurs when the body overheats due to prolonged exposure to high temperatures. It is characterized by symptoms such as sweating, weakness, pallor, and restlessness, but with a roughly normal body temperature. Treatment involves cooling the body, such as by spraying or wiping with cool water. Once recovered, the individual should be able to cautiously move around and, if found, walk out of the woods with assistance.

### Heat Stroke

Heat stroke is a medical emergency characterized by a high body temperature, often without sweating, and changes in consciousness. The body temperature must be rapidly lowered, typically by immersion in a stream or pond. However, caution is needed due to potential changes in consciousness. Alternately, remove clothing and use wet cloths to cool the body. Once cooled, the temperature may rise again, so movement should be limited to signal for help. Evacuation may be necessary.

### Frostbite

Frostbite is caused by low temperature and wind, and is treated with specific methods. The most likely affected areas are the nose, ears, cheeks, fingers, and toes. Early symptoms include flushed skin that later turns white or grayish-yellow. Pain, intense cold, or loss of sensation are warning signs. Treatment involves applying firm pressure with a warm hand or warming the affected part in an armpit. Do not rub the area with the hand or snow, and cover it with dry insulating materials. Drink warm water or soup to help raise body temperature.
