Fear is an emotional response to dangerous circumstances that we believe have the potential to cause death, injury, or illness. It can save a life—or it can cost one. Fear can be both conscious and subconscious, and it varies in intensity, duration, and frequency of occurrence. It may control behavior and can be learned or taught through negative experiences. Fear can lead to reckless behavior if underestimated, and there is a general tendency to underestimate, leading to reckless, foolhardy behavior. Fear can manifest physically through symptoms like quickening of pulse, trembling, and increased muscular tension, and can also manifest psychologically through symptoms like irritability, talkativeness, and speechlessness. The physical and psychological symptoms of fear should be combined into a single section to show how fear affects both the body and mind, and to clarify that they are both manifestations of fear.
