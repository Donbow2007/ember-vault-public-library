Fire Starting Kit

A candle is a common addition to a fire-starting kit, as it easily ignites, burns for a long time, and provides illumination. Before including flint and steel in your survival kit, you should experiment with them to understand their use. A spark is a good backup to flint and steel, as it can be used one-handed in case of injury.

Natural tinder includes dead twigs, wood, moss, leaves, and grass. Birch bark can ignite even when wet. Always start with small pieces and work up to larger materials. As the fire becomes larger and hotter, you can add damp, greener wood. Note: when gathering fuel, a wire or chain-type saw or a small folding handsaw is worth its weight in gold.

The article assumes dry conditions, but more often people become lost on rainy days when clouds hide their landmarks and the sun.
