Hunger is more commonly experienced among survivors than thirst and dehydration. Food aversions may result in hunger, and different groups may have differing opinions about unfamiliar foods. The physical and psychological effects described are reversed when food and a protective environment are restored. The individual tendency is still to search for food to prevent starvation and such efforts might continue as long as strength and self-control permit. When the food supply is limited, even strong friendships are threatened.
