Burns in survival situations are typically minor but should not be ignored. If possible, remove the person from the source of injury and cool the burn with water for 15 to 20 minutes. Do not clean or break blisters. If sterile dressing is available, cover the burn; otherwise, leave it exposed. Do not apply iodine, antiseptics, grease, butter, or cotton. Modern outdoor clothing may melt and cause burns; if clothing is stuck to the burn area, do not remove it. Burn severity depends on depth, location, and size. All full-thickness burns are serious. Burns on the face, neck, genitals, palms, or soles of the feet are considered serious. Burns covering more than one body part, such as the chest or abdomen, are serious. For serious burns, treat for shock in addition to the above recommendations.
