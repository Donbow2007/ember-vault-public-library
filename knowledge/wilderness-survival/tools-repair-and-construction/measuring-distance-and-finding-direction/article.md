Wilderness Survival: Travel In the Woods

When measuring distance, the tally and pace system is the most effective method:

1 pace = 75 cm (29.25 in)
1 double pace = 150 cm or 1.5 m (58.5 in)
66 double paces = 99 m (107.9 yards) = 1 tally (tie a knot in a string for each tally)

10 tallies = 1000 m or 1 km (0.62 mi)

For finding direction:

- Use a magnetic-Sylva compass for lost hikers.
- Aim over the center of the compass to the desired bearing and locate a landmark.
- Repeat the process to follow a straight line.

For those without a compass, the pole star can be used to determine north. Coastal British Columbia compasses read 22 degrees east of true north, so additional time must be allowed for readings in winter.

Stay where you are if lost, build a fire, and avoid wandering aimlessly. Use game trails in winter to avoid deep snow and watch for weak ice on frozen streams and rivers.
