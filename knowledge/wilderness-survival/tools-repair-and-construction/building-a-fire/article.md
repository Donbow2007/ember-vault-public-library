Building a Fire

Building a fire is the most important task when dealing with survival in the wilderness. To build a fire safely, choose a sandy or rocky area or near a supply of sand and water to avoid forest fires. The most common mistakes made by those attempting to build a fire are: choosing poor tinder, failing to shield matches from the wind, and smothering flames with too large pieces of fuel. To build a fire, follow these steps:
1. Gather dry tinder (such as leaves, grass, or small sticks) and kindling.
2. Create a small hole in the ground and place the tinder in it.
3. Light the tinder with a match or a spark.
4. Once the tinder is lit, add small pieces of wood or dry leaves to the hole to sustain the fire.
5. Use a match or a spark to keep the fire going and ensure it is well-ventilated.
6. Once the fire is established, move it to a safer location and monitor it to prevent any hazards.
