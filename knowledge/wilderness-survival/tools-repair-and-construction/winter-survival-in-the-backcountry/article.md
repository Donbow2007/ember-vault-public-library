Winter survival in the backcountry

### Preparation

#### Winter Precautions

- Check all equipment before departing.
- Advise a responsible person of your destination and return time.
- Allow a margin of safety when calculating trip time.
- Take short rest stops for food, water, and toilet breaks.
- Be alert for signs of fatigue, frostbite, sunburn, or other changes in physical condition.
- Watch the weather. Turn back before severe weather conditions develop.
- Carry proper equipment when travelling in avalanche terrain.
- Check the Terrain Classiﬁcation for the area you wish to go and ensure you are adequately prepared.

#### Emergency Services

- Contact the St. John Ambulance or the Canadian Red Cross for CPR courses.

### Shelters

#### Portable Body Shelter

- Use a tarp or canvas.
- Secure to the ground.
- Provide insulation.

#### Rock Shelter

- Find natural shelters.
- Use rocks for insulation.
- Avoid exposure to wind.

#### Snow Shelter

- Create a snow cave or trench.
- Use a tarp for insulation.
- Avoid exposure to wind.

### Frostbite and Hypothermia

#### Frostbite

- Seek medical attention immediately.
- Keep the affected area warm.
- Avoid rubbing the area.

#### Hypothermia

- Keep the person warm.
- Provide shelter.
- Monitor for signs of unconsciousness.

### Avalanches

#### Locating Victims

- Use avalanche transceivers.
- Signal for help.
- Avoid moving victims in snow.

#### Rescue Techniques

- Use a shovel or snow blower.
- Create a safe zone.
- Monitor for signs of life.
